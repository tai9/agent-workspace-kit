# Domain playbook

The reasoning the generic playbooks cannot know: how this product's
category and market actually behave. conversion-audit reads it before
judging gates and paywalls; market-researcher reads it before judging
transferability; product-owner prices its risks into verdicts.

Adoption fills (interviewing the owner, mining prior findings, and
borrowing shamelessly from the category's public post-mortems):

## The value chain, in this category
The ordered links from install to long-term paying user, and which link
products in this category most often lose (in language learning it is
habit formation; name yours).

## What converts here
Which gates and limits drive upgrades in this category vs merely
punish, and where the strong paywall moments sit.

## Unit economics
What serving one active user costs (decisive for AI products), and the
constraint that puts on free tiers.

## The local market
Willingness to pay, payment methods, pricing anchors, cultural traps —
whatever "local" means for this product's actual users.

## Benchmarks worth holding
Category reference numbers (activation, D7/D30, trial→paid) with
sources and dates — labeled as context, never as targets to fake.
